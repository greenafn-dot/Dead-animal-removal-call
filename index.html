Common questions

Can I just move it myself?

On public land, don’t. Beyond the disease risk, an uncollected report means the agency never learns that spot is a wildlife crossing or a rodent problem. On your own property you usually can, but call first — many places require specific bagging, and a rabies vector species may need to be kept for testing.

Why do they ask so many questions?

Species and count feed disease surveillance. Location decides which crew is dispatched and whether they need traffic control. Answering properly the first time is usually the difference between a same-day pickup and a repeat call.

Nobody has come. What now?

Call back with your original reference number. If it has been more than a few days on a public road, escalate to your council member or the department’s supervisor line — persistent reports are how these get prioritised.

I am not in the United States.

The identification and the safety rules hold anywhere. The phone routing does not: 311 is a North American convention. Look up your local council’s environmental health or street cleansing line and save it in the box above.
